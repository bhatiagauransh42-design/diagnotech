---

1. Executive Summary

Diagnotech is an AI-powered health screening platform designed to identify potential risk patterns associated with Diabetes and Cardiovascular Disease (CVD) from structured health and clinical data.

The system combines:

- classical machine learning
- explainable AI
- statistical analysis
- visual health analytics
- model benchmarking
- optional quantum-kernel machine learning

The platform is designed around the principle that an AI health system should not simply answer:

«“Is the patient at risk?”»

It should also answer:

«“Why did the model reach this result?”»

and:

«“How reliable is the model?”»

and:

«“What limitations should be considered?”»

Diagnotech therefore produces a complete screening analysis instead of a simple binary classification.

The platform is intended as a screening and decision-support system, not as a replacement for professional medical diagnosis.

---

2. Problem Statement

Diabetes and cardiovascular diseases are major health challenges where earlier identification of risk can support timely medical evaluation and intervention.

However, many data-driven health systems have significant shortcomings:

- They focus on only one disease.
- They return only a binary prediction.
- They provide little or no explanation.
- They hide model performance from the user.
- They may prioritize accuracy while ignoring recall, specificity, and false-negative cases.
- Users may not understand what a probability or prediction actually means.
- AI predictions are sometimes presented with more certainty than the underlying data supports.
- Different population groups may experience different model performance.

Diagnotech attempts to address these problems by creating a unified, transparent screening platform that combines prediction, explanation, analytics, and responsible AI practices.

3. Product Vision

Vision

«Create an accessible and explainable AI screening system that helps identify potential diabetes and cardiovascular disease risk while making the reasoning and limitations of the AI understandable to users.»

The long-term vision is to develop the platform into a modular health-risk analytics framework capable of supporting additional diseases and datasets.

4. Product Mission

Diagnotech's mission is to transform structured health data into:

Risk Assessment → Explanation → Evidence → Actionable Understanding

without presenting the model as a definitive medical authority.

5. Objectives

5.1 Primary Objectives

Objective 1 — Early Risk Screening

Develop machine-learning models capable of identifying patterns associated with diabetes and cardiovascular disease outcomes.

Objective 2 — Explainability

Provide feature-level explanations for individual predictions.

Objective 3 — Transparency

Display meaningful model-performance metrics rather than hiding them behind a single accuracy score.

Objective 4 — Multi-Disease Architecture

Build a modular platform that can support multiple disease-specific models.

Objective 5 — Comparative AI Research

Compare multiple classical ML models and, where feasible, a quantum-kernel hybrid model.

Objective 6 — User-Friendly Visualization

Convert technical model output into intuitive charts and understandable summaries.

Objective 7 — Responsible AI

Clearly communicate uncertainty, limitations, dataset constraints, and the need for professional medical evaluation.

17. Train/Test Leakage Prevention

Preventing data leakage is a mandatory requirement.

Operations such as:

- imputation
- normalization/scaling
- feature selection

must be fitted using training data only during evaluation.

Recommended structure:

Training Data
     ↓
Fit Preprocessor
     ↓
Train Model

Test Data
     ↓
Transform using fitted preprocessor
     ↓
Predict

The test set must not influence model training or preprocessing decisions.

18. Machine Learning Strategy

Diagnotech should not depend on a single algorithm.

Multiple models should be trained and compared.

Potential candidates include:

Logistic Regression

Useful as an interpretable baseline.

Decision Tree

Useful for understanding simple decision structures.

Random Forest

Useful for nonlinear relationships and feature importance.

Support Vector Machine

Useful for classification in structured feature spaces.

Gradient Boosting / XGBoost

Useful for high-performing tabular-data classification.

The final model should be chosen based on overall evaluation and project requirements, not simply the highest accuracy.

---

19. Model Selection Criteria

The selected model should consider:

- recall/sensitivity
- specificity
- precision
- F1 score
- ROC-AUC
- calibration where appropriate
- interpretability
- stability
- inference speed
- reproducibility

For a health-screening application, the model should not optimize only accuracy.

23. Model Evaluation Metrics

Accuracy

Measures the proportion of correct classifications.

---

Precision

Measures how many predicted positive cases were actually positive.

---

Recall / Sensitivity

Measures how many actual positive cases were identified.

Recall is particularly important in screening applications because false negatives can be significant.

---

Specificity

Measures how well the model identifies negative cases.

---

F1 Score

Balances precision and recall.

---

ROC-AUC

Measures discrimination performance across classification thresholds.

---

Confusion Matrix

The system shall visualize:

                 Actual
              Positive  Negative

Predicted +
Predicted -

The dashboard should clearly distinguish:

- true positives
- true negatives
- false positives
- false negatives

40. Privacy Requirements

The platform should follow privacy-by-design principles.

The system should:

- minimize collection of personal information
- avoid collecting unnecessary identifiers
- encrypt data in transit
- secure APIs
- restrict administrative access
- avoid logging sensitive health information unnecessarily
- secure model files
- prevent unauthorized prediction access where authentication is implemented

For an SIH prototype, demonstration data should preferably be controlled, synthetic, or appropriately anonymized rather than unnecessary real patient information.

---

41. Fairness & Bias Analysis

The project should evaluate whether model performance varies across relevant population subgroups when sufficient demographic information exists.

Possible measures include:

- recall by subgroup
- specificity by subgroup
- false-negative rate
- precision by subgroup
- calibration
- overall accuracy

The platform should explicitly document dataset limitations.

---

42. Dataset Generalization

A model that performs well on a benchmark dataset does not automatically perform equally well in real-world populations.

Therefore, the project must distinguish between:

Demonstrated Performance

Performance on the selected evaluation dataset.

and:

Real-World Clinical Performance

Performance that would require external validation using appropriate independent clinical data.

The system must not claim clinical deployment readiness without appropriate validation.

60. Success Criteria

Product Success

A user should be able to:

1. select Diabetes or Cardiovascular Disease
2. enter the required information
3. receive a model prediction
4. view a probability/risk estimate
5. understand the main contributing features
6. inspect model performance
7. understand the limitations of the prediction

---

ML Success

The project should demonstrate:

- properly separated training/testing data
- reproducible preprocessing
- multiple model comparisons
- appropriate evaluation metrics
- explainable predictions
- model versioning
- transparent limitations

---

SIH Success

The solution should demonstrate that it is more than a basic:

«“Train model → predict 0/1”»

application.

The intended value proposition is:

              DIAGNOTECH

        ┌──────────────────┐
        │    Prediction    │
        └────────┬─────────┘
                 ↓
        ┌──────────────────┐
        │   Explanation    │
        └────────┬─────────┘
                 ↓
        ┌──────────────────┐
        │    Analytics     │
        └────────┬─────────┘
                 ↓
        ┌──────────────────┐
        │   Transparency   │
        └────────┬─────────┘
                 ↓
        ┌──────────────────┐

        └──────────────────┘

62. Final Product Positioning

Diagnotech should be positioned as:

«An explainable, multi-disease AI screening and health analytics platform.»

It should not be positioned as:

«“An AI doctor.”»

Its strength lies in combining:

Machine Learning + Explainability + Quantitative Evaluation + Visualization + Responsible AI

into one workflow.

---

63. Final SIH Pitch

One-Line Pitch

«Diagnotech is an explainable AI-powered screening platform that estimates diabetes and cardiovascular disease risk from structured health data, explains the factors behind each prediction, transparently evaluates model reliability, and benchmarks classical machine learning against a quantum-kernel approach.»

Core Innovation

«Diagnotech does not stop at predicting risk — it explains the prediction and shows how trustworthy the underlying model is.»

Core Pipeline

«Health Data → AI Prediction → Explainability → Model Evidence → Human Decision Support»

---

64. Product Success Principle

The ultimate success of Diagnotech is not simply:

«“Can AI predict disease?”»

It is:

«“Can AI provide a useful, understandable, measurable, and responsibly communicated early-risk screening result?”»

That principle should guide the dataset selection, model development, backend architecture, UI design, evaluation strategy, and final SIH presentation.
